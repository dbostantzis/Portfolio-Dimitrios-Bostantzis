2021

Aerodynamic design of a Formula Student race car

Context
Aristotle Racing Team, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, 2021. Team project.
My role
Aerodynamics team leader, CAD, CFD, manufacturing and testing
Tools
CATIA v5, ANSA/META (BETA CAE Systems), ANSYS Fluent

Design, CFD optimisation, manufacturing and track validation of the full aerodynamic package of the Aristotle Racing Team 2021 car.

Problem and approach

  • Goal: Best CL–CD–weight combination for Formula SAE tracks, with sufficient cooling for engine and radiators.
  • Aeropack selection: Lap-time simulator and tyre friction ellipse, comparing the car with and without wings.
  • Airfoils: Custom designed profiles with control of the leading edge, suction and pressure sides and the suction peak.
  • CFD model: Half-car, ~40 million cells (y+ 1–10, 15 layers, 0.45–15 mm), 15.56 m/s, rotating wheels, moving road, Spalart–Allmaras and k-ω SST.
  • Optimisation: Parametric studies with the ANSA morphing tool: aeromap (15 cases), DRS (20 cases).
  • Build and test: Carbon-fibre parts, then on-car experiments to validate the CFD.

Key results

  • +87%diffuser downforce vs. the 2017 front wing (+54% radiator mass flow)
  • 2–2.5 sfaster in a 43 s lap with the aero package vs. no wings
  • +38%downforce from the firewall engine cover (+18% from the curved endplate)

Design highlights

  • Front wing: 3D-printed curved wingtips create an outwashing vortex that displaces the tyre wake and feeds the diffuser and radiators.
  • Undertray and diffuser: Convex curve for smoother pressure recovery, two venturi tunnels, strakes, middle plate and footplates.
  • Rear wing: Optimal CL-to-weight with low drag; curved endplate (inwash effect) and firewall cover that also cools the engine and air intake.
  • Sidepods: Designed for maximum radiator mass flow, using the front-wing vortex to shield the inlet.

Manufacturing and validation

  • Manufacturing: Joggle joint chosen over a lap joint (smooth leading edge, bonding area, easier lay-up); aluminium ribs for glue-free assembly, carbon ribs with aluminium inserts for the front wing.
  • Flow visualisation: Paraffin and titanium dioxide solution compared with the CFD results.

What I learned

  • Leadership: Turning a large technical scope into clear tasks and keeping the team aligned under deadlines.
  • CFD practice: Mesh quality decides whether vortices are captured; combining two turbulence models balances their strengths.
  • Design to reality: Linking simulation, manufacturing and testing, and being honest about the gap between CFD and measurements.

2025

CFD analysis of a wing-engine nacelle configuration

Context
University coursework, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, 2025. Individual.
My role
CAD modelling, geometry preparation, meshing, Fluent set-up, post-processing
Tools
Inventor, ANSA/META (BETA CAE Systems), ANSYS Fluent

A compressible CFD study of the flow field and pressure distribution around a wing with an engine nacelle.

Problem and approach

  • Problem: Investigate the flow field and pressure distribution.
  • Model: CAD model of the wing and nacelle.
  • Mesh: Volume mesh with local refinements (33 million cells, y+ = 1).
  • Solver: Density-based (implicit), viscous k-ω SST.
  • Post-processing: Mach number, CL, CD and CP contour visualisation.

What I learned

  • Sizing the mesh from the physics rather than by trial and error.
  • Geometry cleanup: removing small gaps and surfaces.
  • Domain size: far-field placed far enough from the wing to avoid boundary influence.
  • Local refinement near the nacelle lip, pylon junction and wake.
  • Choice of turbulence model and its y+ requirement.
  • Discretisation: second-order schemes for accuracy, a first-order start for stability.

2022–23

Dynamic analysis of a vehicle frame: FEM and numerical integration

Context
Numerical Methods in Mechanical Vibrations, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, 2022–23. Two linked assignments.
My role
Geometry cleanup, meshing, FE set-up and analyses; MATLAB assembly and time integration
Tools
HyperMesh, OptiStruct, HyperView, MATLAB

Finite-element model of a welded tubular vehicle frame with four suspension–wheel subsystems, solved in HyperWorks/OptiStruct and re-implemented in MATLAB for verification.

Problem and approach

  • Model: S355 steel tubular frame (shell elements) on solid corner brackets, with four suspension–wheel subsystems (CELAS2, CDAMP2, CONM2) and ground excitation at each wheel.
  • Meshing: Geometry cleanup and part (PID) organisation; CQUAD/CHEXA and CTRIA/CTETRA meshes at 10–30 mm, compared on modes and static response.
  • FE analyses: 15 modes; static load through RBE3; modal transient (ζ = 0.01) and direct transient with Rayleigh damping (α = 0.074, β = 0.00026) plus an impact force, 2 s at 0.001 s.
  • MATLAB verification: Assembled the 70×70 M, K, C matrices from the reduced 66×66 frame and the four 2-DOF subsystems, solved the eigenproblem, and integrated with Newmark and central differences.

Key results

  • 15modes extracted (3 rigid-body, 12 elastic)
  • ≤ 3%MATLAB vs. HyperWorks, modes 4–10 (2.5–10.6 Hz)
  • IdenticalNewmark vs. central-difference responses

What I learned

Displacements agree across all three methods; differences grow for velocity and acceleration and for higher modes, so high-frequency quantities need more care.

2024

Conceptual design of a 4-seat light aircraft (UD-1): static stability

Context
Aircraft Design & Control, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, 2024. Team of 4.
My role
Longitudinal static stability: wing and tail moment contributions, trim, neutral point
Tools
XFLR5, MATLAB, Anderson methods

Team design of a single-engine aircraft, from weight estimate to performance. My focus: longitudinal static stability of the wing–tail configuration.

Problem and approach

  • Aircraft: 2 crew + 4 passengers, 235 hp piston engine, 1,300 km range, 300 m take-off and landing. Final mass 2,448 kg, wing span 14.2 m, area 28.4 m², AR 7.15, taper 0.5.
  • Stability model: Pitching-moment equation about the CG for the wing and for the horizontal tail (downwash ε, tail efficiency η, tail volume VHT = 0.7), combined into Cm(α).
  • Findings: The wing alone is unstable (Cm,α > 0); the tail makes the aircraft stable (Cm,α < 0) with Cm0 > 0, so it can trim at positive angles of attack.
  • Neutral point: Located about 3.8 m from the fuselage nose, almost 1 m behind the CG with full fuel, which sets how far the CG may move aft.

Key results

  • Cm,α < 0stable with the tail; the wing alone is unstable
  • ≈ 1 mneutral point behind the CG (full fuel)
  • 2,448 kgfinal take-off mass

What I learned and next step

A tail is what turns an unstable wing into a stable aircraft, and the neutral point sets the allowed CG range. The design meets the requirements only partly (max speed about 230 km/h vs. 280 km/h target, landing distance well above 300 m). Next step: more engine power and a revised wing area to balance take-off and landing.

2023

Digital state-space control of a nonlinear 2-DOF oscillator

Context
University coursework (Automatic & Digital Control of Dynamic Systems), Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, 2023. Individual.
Tools
MATLAB / Simulink

Estimation and digital control of the states of a two-mass oscillator with cubic springs and damping.

Problem and approach

  • Problem: Estimate and control the states of a 2-DOF oscillator with cubic (nonlinear) springs and damping around an operating point.
  • Modelling: Taylor linearisation around the operating point, state-space model in deviation variables, discretised with a sampling period chosen from the Bode resonances (Shannon criterion).
  • Estimation: Prediction estimator and current (synchronous) observer by pole placement, with error dynamics an order of magnitude faster than open loop. Compared on the linear and on the nonlinear process (ode45).
  • Control: State feedback by pole placement for a 3× faster response with overshoot < 20%, combined with the estimator; integral action added to remove steady-state error under disturbance.

What I learned

A design made on the linear model must be checked on the nonlinear one. The current observer uses the latest measurement and converges faster than the prediction estimator. Pure state feedback leaves a steady-state error under disturbance, so integral action is needed; pole placement on augmented models needs careful pole choice.
